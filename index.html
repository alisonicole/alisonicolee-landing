Why tokens in gold

Worth your weight in gold, updated for AI.

The phrase came to me after I built my first app with AI. I'm not technical, and I'd made something that actually worked. The tokens it used cost almost nothing. The feeling of realizing I was capable of building it was priceless.

Every time you use AI, it spends tokens: the small chunks of text a model reads and writes. They're how AI gets priced, and they keep getting cheaper. AI put a token price on every task. The people who stay worth more than that price are the ones who know how to put it to work.

So the old compliment needed an update. Worth your tokens in gold means getting back more than you spend. That's what I write about, and what I help people do.

"The gap isn't between people who can use AI and people who can't. It's between people who know what to ask and people who are just prompting into the void."

The path

Pivot points

Sales, then strategy, then sideways into AI. Almost every door along the way, someone opened for me.

  1. 2018

    P&G

    I wanted to learn sales, because every business runs on it, and P&G was the best training ground there was. It was also the first stock my grandmother ever bought.

    I built an Excel tool that turned a report that took hours into one that took minutes. Fifty people ended up using it. I didn't call it building yet.

  2. 2022

    Wharton

    I wanted more than my slice of CPG. I liked building the strategy for my customers, and I wanted to see more scenarios than one category could give me.

    Door: my managers, who wrote my recommendations
  3. 2024

    BCG

    I wanted to see every industry and every function. Consulting taught me how to break a problem down, act on it, and hold my own in the rooms where the big decisions get made.

    Door: my classmates, who prepped me for every case
  4. 2026

    Sideways into AI

    Someone close to me built an app with Claude Code to help friends find time to get together. I signed up that day and built my first app on the side. When I showed it to Thomas Cerenov, a colleague at BCG, he said, “you know I do this at BCG, right?” I had no idea that was an option.

    I joined BCG's Applied AI team, shipped an agent now required on ~500 cases a year, and was promoted to Applied AI Lead, then Project Leader.

    Doors: the person who showed me Claude Code, and Thomas
  5. 2026

    Scribe

    I posted about becoming Applied AI Lead, and startups started reaching out. I left consulting because I wanted to be at an AI-native company, with people working at the frontier, in an operator role where I could have real impact.

    Door: this one I opened myself. I posted my work.

Every move that worked, I made for a reason that matched what I value. Scribe was autonomy and impact.

Four of these doors, someone opened for me. Now I hold them open.
